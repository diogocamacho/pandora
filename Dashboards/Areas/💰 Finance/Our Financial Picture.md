# Our Financial Picture

*How Diogo and Allison think about our money. This is the money we manage ourselves. It does not cover the Waymark accounts, which they run for us, or our 401(k)s. We review this together once a month.*

---

## The short version

We earn a good living. We also carry a lot of debt and we live a full life, so most of each paycheck is already spoken for before it arrives. When there's a gap, it has quietly been filled by dipping into savings, which is why saving has felt impossible even on a strong income.

The plan here fixes that in stages. First we kill the expensive credit cards. Then we build a real safety net. Then, as our car loans end, we keep that money instead of spending it. Each stage frees up more room than the last. The long-term goal is financial independence: enough saved and invested that work becomes something we choose, not something we need.

Nothing here requires us to be perfect. It requires us to follow a few simple rules and check in once a month.

---

## Where the money comes from

Together we bring home about **$26,000 a month** after taxes. Diogo is paid on the 15th and the last day of each month. Allison is paid every week. Once a year, in March, Diogo gets a bonus, which we treat separately (see "Vacations and camp" below). We never count on the bonus to cover the basics.

---

## How we run the household: three buckets

Every paycheck is split three ways. It's a simple, well-known rule called **50/30/20**:

- **50% → Bills.** The fixed, must-pay things: mortgage, car loans, the home-equity loan, insurance, utilities, phone. These run on autopay.
- **30% → Checking.** Everyday spending we do with the debit card: groceries, gas, dining, kids' activities, Amazon, the club.
- **20% → Savings.** The bucket that builds our future. Right now it's paying off debt. Soon it will build our safety net. Later it will invest.

Three accounts, three jobs. The whole system works because each account's money matches its purpose.

---

## The honest truth about right now

Our bills actually take about **58%** of what we earn, not 50%, because of how much debt we carry. That 8% difference is roughly **$2,225 a month**, and until now it's been coming out of savings without us really noticing.

So two things are true today:

1. **Our real savings rate is closer to 13%, not 20%.** That's temporary. It climbs back up as we pay off debt.
2. **To keep the math honest, Diogo moves $1,200 from Savings into Bills on each of his two paydays.** That covers the gap and leaves a small cushion in Bills for expensive winter months (heating oil, electric). The exact "how" lives in the Paycheck Flow Cheat Sheet.

This is the single most important thing to understand: we are not broke, and we are not overspending wildly. We are **debt-heavy**, and debt is eating the room we'd otherwise save. Fix the debt, and the savings appear on their own.

## What we owe, and the order we pay it

Here's everything, most expensive first. The rule is simple: **attack the most expensive debt, pay only the minimum on everything else.**

| What | Roughly | Interest rate | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| JetBlue credit card | $9,800 | **29.74%** | The emergency. Paid off first. |
| Fidelity credit card | $5,340 | **17.49%** | Paid off second. |
| Audi loan | ~$24,000 | 5.25% | ~24 payments left, done around Oct 2028. |
| Volvo loan | (to confirm) | 5.49% | — |
| Home equity loan (HELOC) | — | 7.0% | ~$1,100/mo, fixed schedule. |
| Mortgage | ~$880,000 | 5.25% | Our home is worth ~$1.3M, so we hold ~$420k of equity. |
| 0% repair loans (HVAC, plumbing) | — | 0% | No rush. Never prepay a 0% loan while a 29.74% card exists. |

Why the credit cards first: **29.74% is punishing.** Every dollar we put toward that card is like earning a guaranteed 29.74% return, which beats anything we could invest in. While we carry a card balance, there's no grace period, so any new charge starts collecting interest immediately. That's why, until the cards are gone, **we don't put anything new on them.** Everyday spending goes on the debit card, and we keep a buffer in a separate account for surprises so nothing forces us back onto a card.

## Our safety net: the emergency fund

This is the money that lets us sleep at night and ride out anything: a job change, a medical surprise, a roof.

- **Target: about $100,000**, which is six months of our full spending.
- **At least half of it ($50,000) stays in plain cash** (a high-yield savings account), never invested, so it's there even if the market is down.
- **Where we are today: about $15,000.** Thin for a household our size, which is why the safety net is the first thing savings builds once the cards are gone.

We will not touch the cash part to chase an investment or a dip. Its whole job is to be boring and available.

---

## The plan, in four phases

**Phase 1 — Kill the cards (now through about spring 2027).**
All of our savings, roughly $2,850 a month, goes at the credit cards: JetBlue first, then Fidelity. Our cash stays about flat during this time, and that's fine. Flat cash plus $15,000 less debt means we're $15,000 richer, at a guaranteed 29.74%. This year's vacations are covered by the bonus so they don't compete with the payoff.

**Phase 2 — Build the safety net (2027 to 2028).**
The moment the cards hit zero, that same ~$2,850 a month flips to building the cash safety net toward $50,000, then on toward $100,000.

**Phase 3 — The cars roll off (2028 and beyond).**
When the Audi is paid off (around October 2028) and later the Volvo, that's about **$2,231 a month** that stops going to car payments. This is the big unlock. We keep that money instead of spending it, which pushes our bills back down to 50% and our savings back up to a true 20% and beyond.

**Phase 4 — Freedom.**
With no cards, no car payments, a full safety net, and a rising savings rate, our money starts compounding for us. This is the on-ramp to financial independence and to Diogo's move toward a C-level role being a choice we can afford to make on our terms.

## The fun stuff: vacations and camp

We love our trips and camp, and they stay. Martha's Vineyard, Westerly, spring and fall vacations, and Concord Academy camp run about **$37,000 a year**. Here's how we pay for all of it without a credit card:

- **The March bonus funds the year's lifestyle.** When it lands, we move the whole lifestyle amount into a separate savings account in one transfer.
- **We spend down from that account** as each trip and camp bill comes due, moving money to checking when we need it.
- **Nothing lands on a credit card.** The cash is already set aside before the bill arrives.

One honest note: at $37,000 a year, these trips are a real choice, not a small one. For now the bonus covers them comfortably. The day we'd like them to be funded without the bonus is the day the car loans roll off and free up the room. Until then, the bonus is their home, on purpose.

---

## Two golden rules

These two habits matter more than any coupon or cancelled subscription:

1. **Bank every raise.** When either of us gets a raise, the extra take-home goes straight to Savings, not to a bigger lifestyle. We set up our paychecks so this happens automatically.
2. **When a loan ends, keep the money.** When a car is paid off, we do not go buy a nicer car and start a new payment. We keep that ~$1,000+ a month working for us. Breaking the car-payment habit even once is worth more to our future than years of small cuts.

Lifestyle creep is the quiet thing that keeps high earners from ever feeling ahead. These two rules are how we beat it.

## What we don't touch

- **Waymark accounts** (~$1M): they manage these. Not part of this plan.
- **Our 401(k)s:** we keep contributing; the advisor guides strategy.
- **Home equity** (~$420k): real, but it's our house, not spending money.

## Our monthly money date

Twenty minutes, once a month, the two of us. We check:

1. **Did we follow the paycheck flow?** Transfers made, no new card charges.
2. **Credit cards:** did the balances go down? By how much? Are we on track for payoff?
3. **The four everyday numbers:** groceries, dining + takeout, gas, Amazon. Any creeping up?
4. **Kids' app store and gaming:** our easiest leak to plug.
5. **Safety net:** what's our cash balance, and what's the gap to the next milestone?
6. **Anything changed?** A raise, a bill that jumped, a loan ending, a trip to fund.
7. **One decision or action** for the month ahead.

The point isn't to be graded. It's to catch small drifts early and to keep each other honest, together.
