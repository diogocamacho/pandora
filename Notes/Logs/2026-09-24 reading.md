## What stuck
- The narrator's constant need for validation and human affection — seeking intimacy everywhere while unable to hold onto what's closest. He doesn't remember his own daughter.
- The rawness of the prose. Lobo Antunes doesn't protect you from the narrator's contradictions.

## My pushback / questions
- Nothing to steal stylistically. But the book reads differently when it's not abstract.

## Resonance
- Connects directly to Diogo's father, who served in the Mozambique front in the early-to-mid 1970s (same Portuguese Colonial War, different theater). The rawness of what Lobo Antunes describes — the dissociation, the need for affection, the war's psychological cost — illuminates what Diogo only partially understood about his father's experience. Not from what his father said, but from what he didn't.
