- **Wet-lab validation is the dividing line in generative protein design.** [[BindCraft one-shot binder design (Pacesa 2025)]] and [[BoltzGen — Universal Binder Design (Stark 2025)]] both earned a strong verdict on experimentally confirmed binders (BoltzGen reached nanomolar on 66% of genuinely novel targets across eight independent labs), while [[Proteo-R1 Reasoning-Guided De Novo Protein Design (Wu 2026)]] stayed mixed with zero wet-lab. The Adaptyv TREM2 hackathon ([[Can LLMs design proteins?]]), where autonomous agents tied human teams on hit rate, sits between the two: a real signal about the design layer, not yet about the biology.
- **The metric itself is increasingly what gets attacked.** [[All that structure matches does not glitter (Martirossyan 2025)]] (strong) shows the standard crystal-structure benchmarks are leakage-contaminated and the match-rate is gameable; [[Karenina multi-dimensional biomedical AI evaluation (Carli 2026)]] shows a lone correctness score hides sycophancy and confident reversals; [[Fifteen Challenges for Generative AI in Cell Biology (Dupire 2026)]] argues the whole subfield stalls because it chases retrospective benchmarks. Three independent sources, one indictment.
- **Expert oversight keeps surfacing as the actual moat, not the model.** [[Rethinking Bioinformatics Expertise in the AI Era (Goh 2026)]] frames AI as an accelerant that "democratizes the appearance of science" without expertise to steer it, and the HBR cluster ([[Research Why You Shouldn’t Treat AI Agents Like Employees]], [[To Thrive Alongside AI, Focus on Mindset—Not Skillset]]) converges on redesigning workflows so humans stay accountable rather than treating agents as staff. This is the spine of [[2026-10-06 agentic-ai-judgment-not-automation]].
- **Nanopore protein reading: identification is demonstrated, de novo sequencing still is not.** [[Multi-pass single-molecule nanopore reading of protein strands (Motone 2024)]] and the [[Advancing nanopore technology toward protein identification and sequencing (Rukes 2025)]] review both draw that line cleanly. The ML layer is genuinely real on raw signal ([[SquiggleNet — direct CNN classification of nanopore signals (Bao 2021)]], [[Nanopore base calling on the edge — DeepNano-coral (Peresini 2021)]]), but the headline peptide-classification accuracy ([[Deep learning classification of peptide nanopore translocations (Krantz 2025)]]) is measured mostly on simulated data.
- **The reality gap is a structural feature, not a bug to be patched.** [[Simulation and the reality gap — a prehistory of synthetic data (Steinhoff 2025)]] argues every simulation needs real-world data to set its parameters, so the gap never closes. That reframes the nanopore and ML papers reporting near-perfect accuracy on simulated squiggles: the number describes the simulator, the same circularity flagged in [[AI Nutrition Recommendation via VAE and ChatGPT (Papastratis 2024)]], where accuracy was scored against the rule-based generator that made the training data.
