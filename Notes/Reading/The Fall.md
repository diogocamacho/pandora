## 🧵 Threads & themes
- Judge vs. judged: reader starts out judging the narrator, then flips to feeling judged. The narrator announces "judge-penitent" early, and it still works.
- Direct address to "you" as the mechanism that implicates the reader.
- Earnest confession vs. performance: does the self-accusation ever cost him anything?
- The bridge scene as the hinge: judging him for walking past her, then catching myself rationalizing the same way ("what would I have done? she probably just wants to be alone"). The difference between us is that he already told me who he is.

## 💬 Discussion
- 2026-10-01 — Why is an unlikable narrator still readable, and does "you" make me feel judged? Me: I feel like I'm judging him, not the other way around.
- 2026-10-02 — After the pivot, is he condemning himself in earnest or staying in control of the verdict? Me: cautious, wondering if it's earnest or all for show.
- 2026-10-05 — On finishing: Camus pivots the reader from judging the protagonist to feeling judged. Unexpected, even though he announces "judge-penitent" early on.
- 2026-10-05 — The girl on the bridge was the turn: I judged him for walking by, then asked what I'd have done and assumed she probably wanted to be alone. That's when I realized I may not be much different from Clamence; he just told me up front who he is.
- 2026-10-05 (cont.) — Unsure Camus endorses universal guilt, but he puts it out there that we're not as guilt-free as we think. Are we that different from Clamence, or do we just keep it inside and never confess? Would we turn around for the girl? Would we admit misogyny or racism? Why hold him to a higher bar if we never admit guilt to anything? Maybe Clamence uses it to say "I'm off the hook, you're not," but there's a deeper point about the guilt-free human as a whole.
- 2026-10-05 (cont.) — Clamence isn't hiding, but that doesn't make him more honest. I got very angry at what he confesses; it's grounds for complete objection to him and his entitled point of view. Maybe his honesty is exactly that: he puts it out in the open.
- 2026-10-05 (cont.) — The anger didn't fade after the pivot. It stayed, and it also left me asking what I would have done differently, if anything.
