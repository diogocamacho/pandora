## Team & reporting structure — the part that matters most

- CTO is **Kristóf Szalay, PhD** — co-founder, invented Turbine's original cell-simulation method, and per public bio still "leads research efforts for Virtual Assays development." That's the exact scope the VP of AI is being hired to run.
- CSO is **Daniel Véres, MD/PhD** — co-founder, owns the biology/clinical-translation layer.
- This means the VP of AI is being inserted into a company where the technical vision and the biology interpretation are both already owned by co-founders who built the thing. The organizational question isn't "can Diogo do the job" — it's "what's actually being delegated here, and how much room does a founder-CTO give a VP two years after inventing the core method himself."
- This is worth probing directly and early, not diplomatically: is this a real delegation of technical ownership, or a management/scaling layer under a CTO who keeps the interesting calls. Ask Kris directly what decisions he's giving up.

## The CAIO question — direct read

You said what you want is a clear path to CAIO. This role works against that thesis as structured:

- **Reports to CTO, not CEO.** A VP of AI under a co-founder CTO is one more rung from the board/CEO conversation than the Head of Computational Sciences seat you already hold reports-wise — you'd be trading "Head of function reporting to a hiring manager at a Flagship venture" for "VP reporting to a co-founder CTO who isn't going anywhere." At an 85–100 person company, the CTO seat isn't opening up, and there's no CAIO seat currently open above it to grow into — you'd effectively be capped below a founder for the tenure of your stay.
- **Scope is narrower than "Chief AI Officer" implies.** The mandate is Virtual Assay / virtual-cell AI specifically, not enterprise AI strategy across the company (commercial, ops, etc.) the way a CAIO title usually implies. It's a strong technical-leadership role, but it's VP-of-a-function, not C-suite-of-the-company.
- If CAIO trajectory is the non-negotiable, the question to ask Turbine directly (not softened) is: "Is there a CAIO or Chief AI/Science seat on this company's org chart in the next 2–3 years, and would this role be the funnel into it, or is VP of AI the ceiling under the current CTO?" If the honest answer is "VP is the ceiling," this role should be evaluated purely on comp/scope/learning, not on the CAIO thesis — don't let the title's proximity to "AI" do the work of a real answer.

## Team & culture — insularity risk, not a nationality point

- Founding exec team (CEO Szabolcs Nagy, CTO Kristóf Szalay, CSO Daniel Véres, CFO Máté Zsombok) is Hungarian and has run this one company together since 2015 — a decade-old Budapest University spinout. The board/investor side is the normal international VC mix (independent directors, IVP/Mercia/Accel observers, a Merck rep) — that's a real external check, but it doesn't touch day-to-day culture.
- The substantive risk isn't nationality, it's that the entire leadership core has only ever worked inside this one company. No one at the top has a reference point for how other orgs run things, and the JD's own language ("coach and develop your department to operate seamlessly as part of a modern product organization") is effectively an admission they aren't one yet.
- Given Diogo's pattern (5 different company cultures, low tolerance for sloppiness/unexamined habits, skeptical-empiricism default), the likely friction is: being the first outsider telling a founder who invented the core method himself that his group's way of operating needs to change. Founders in that position are typically the slowest to hear it, not the fastest — compounds directly with the CTO-delegation risk already flagged above.
- De-risking move: don't vibe-check the team — ask Kris directly for one specific example of him deferring to someone else's operating call in the last two years. No example = the delegation isn't real.
- Also worth a gut-check against the hype-vs-substance test: public language ("virtual cell," "2x faster," "1.5x success rate," "zero-shot assay prediction") runs hot for a decade-old company on a modest $25M round. Worth probing internally whether leadership believes its own pitch at that same intensity, or whether there's a more sober operating reality underneath — if it's the former, that wears on Diogo fast.

## Comp — basis and posture

- Current base is $339k. The $380–400k figure is *your* ask, not a market benchmark and not a make-whole calculation — that make-whole logic (base + 4% COL + forfeited bonus as sign-on) only applies to staying inside the Flagship ecosystem (e.g., Tilde), where the counterfactual is "not really leaving." Turbine is outside Flagship — there's no parity tether protecting a specific number, so $380–400k is an opening anchor for an aggressive negotiation, not a floor to defend on arithmetic.
- Bonus (20–25%) is standard VP-band; nothing unusual there.
- Equity is the wildcard, and it's also the likeliest lever they'll lean on to keep base from moving: "sizeable" means nothing without share count, strike price, and last-round (Series B) valuation. Watch for a pattern where they hold base near the low end of your range and try to make up the difference with equity talk late in the conversation — treat that as a negotiating move, not a generous offer, and push for both the number and the equity detail rather than letting one substitute for the other.
- This is a modestly-capitalized platform company relative to categories like Recursion, Insitro, or Isomorphic — not a knock, but it's a reason to get equity specifics in writing rather than take "sizeable" at face value, especially given your priorities around debt paydown and liquidity.

## Watch-outs to raise directly with Kris / in the case study

1. What specifically will you own that Kris currently owns himself — get this in writing/specifics, not generalities.
2. Where does this role sit if/when Turbine raises a Series C and adds a CAIO or Chief Science seat — would you be the incumbent or would that seat be brought in above you.

The "virtual cell" / mechanistic simulation core tech is a real domain gap versus your multi-omics/protein-design/active-learning background — don't undersell this in the technical conversation; name it and pivot to how fast you close analogous gaps (you did this moving into D-amino/Synteins at Abio in under a year).
4. 60% of the team is in Budapest; you'd be hired for US East or UK. Ask how technical leadership actually works across that time zone/culture split day to day — this is an operating-model question, not a courtesy one.
5. AI-conference visibility ("nice to have," representing Turbine externally) is a genuine gap relative to candidates coming from pure AI/ML backgrounds — your visibility is real but pharma/board-facing, not NeurIPS/ICML-facing. Worth a straight answer if asked, not overclaiming it.

## Net

Take the meeting, go in technically confident (this is squarely in your wheelhouse on the platform-strategy and team-building axes), and use the first conversation with Kris to pressure-test the CAIO thesis and the delegation question before investing more time. If the answer on both is weak, this is still a legitimate comp-and-scope move worth having in the search — just not the C-suite pathway you're optimizing for.
