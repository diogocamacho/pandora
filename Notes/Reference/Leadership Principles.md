# 🧭 Leadership Principles

## The throughline

Every source converges on one arc: **self → others → organization**. You cannot lead a team you don't understand. You cannot lead an organization without a team that trusts you. And none of it starts anywhere but inside.

The seven transformations (Opportunist → Diplomat → Expert → Achiever → Individualist → Strategist → Alchemist) map onto this arc. Most technical leaders plateau at **Expert** or **Achiever** — excellent at doing, less comfortable enabling. The leverage jump is Achiever → Strategist: shifting from "I am the answer" to "I build the conditions for the answer."

---

## What actually sticks

**Listen first, always.**
Not as politeness — as a leadership instrument. Listening is the only way to know what's actually happening vs. what gets reported. It gives your team voice, which is the precondition for trust. If you're talking more than listening in a 1:1, you're doing it wrong.

**Trust is built in small actions, lost in one.**
Walk the talk. Share credit widely. Share the pain. Get your hands dirty when it matters. These aren't gestures — they're the ledger your team keeps. Authenticity means being open, showing humanity, and being humble. Confidence and competence shine through that, not despite it.

**Be decisive, not stubborn.**
The collaborative mode: gather real input, then make the decision yourself and own it. If you change a decision to appease others, you've lost authority. Explain every decision — good explanations make outcomes feel just even when people disagree.

**Your first job is making your people successful.**
Servant leadership is practical, not philosophical. Commit to professional and personal growth for your reports. Develop a lieutenant — someone who can step in, give you unfiltered feedback, and is emphatically NOT a yes-man. Their job is to surface what you can't see from your seat.

**Follow through relentlessly.**
This appears in every note. It's the most common leadership failure. Make fewer commitments, keep all of them.

**Candor over comfort.**
Be specific in feedback — vague feedback is useless. Deliver it positively even when it's difficult. The cost of not saying the thing is higher than the discomfort of saying it.

**Change starts with you.**
You cannot drive change top-down and have it stick. Model it. Be the behavior you want. DEI = diversity of ideas. Groups are awkward before they're great — that's the process.

## Where to grow

Based on the seven transformations, the move from Achiever → Individualist → Strategist involves:
- Questioning systems, not just executing within them
- Leading through vision rather than personal performance
- Holding complexity without needing to resolve it immediately

The friction point is usually letting go of being the expert in the room.
