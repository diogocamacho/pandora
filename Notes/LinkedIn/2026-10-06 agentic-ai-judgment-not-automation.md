Most of the agentic AI effort in drug discovery is aimed at the wrong problem.

The consensus is that agents are good at execution, so we point them at execution. Run the pipeline faster, automate the assay, clear the queue. That is useful, and it is still the same work at higher throughput.

The problems that move a program are judgment problems. Which disease to go after, which modality to bet on, whether the data we already have is being used at all. Those decide whether a drug ends up better than what patients already take, and they are the calls we are least comfortable handing to a model.

There is a bias worth naming. The field reaches for new data types, spatial transcriptomics, single-cell proteomics, because they are fundable, publishable, and easy to demo. Not because the old data was exhausted. Decades of microarray, bulk RNA-seq, and clinical records still carry signal, and the methods are finally good enough to pull more of it out.

My bet is that some of the next targets are sitting in data the field already walked past.

This is not an argument against new modalities. It is an argument against treating novelty as the default place the answer lives. Point the models at the judgment calls, re-mine what you already paid for, and be honest about which bet is cheaper to run.
